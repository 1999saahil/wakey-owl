The Birthplace of Indian Coffee
Bababudangiri Cloud Ridge • Chikmagalur, Karnataka
1670 AD

The Seven Sacred Seeds

While returning from a pilgrimage to Mecca through the Yemeni port of Mocha, the Sufi saint Baba Budan strapped seven live green coffee beans across his chest.

He planted them in the volcanic soils of the Chandragiri Hills of Chikmagalur. Those seven seeds established India as the first nation outside the Arab world to cultivate coffee.

1888 AD

The High-Altitude Sovereign: Attikan

Pioneering planters scaled the sacred Biligirirangana Hills (BR Hills) at 1,750m—surrounded by wild elephants and tigers.

The extreme sub-zero nocturnal chill and dense fog yielded the legendary Attikan Estate SLN 79.5, famed for intense cacao density, sweet Mysore jaggery notes, and zero astringency.

1920s AD

The Royal Mysore Court Ritual

Under the patronage of the Wadiyar Dynasty of Mysore, the traditional brass Davarah filter kaapi became the hallmark of regal hospitality.

The royal formula specified 85% Peaberry Arabica and 15% slow-roasted Jamnagar chicory, frothed into a golden crest using hand-hammered pure brass vessels.

The Western Ghats Canopy

Why Indian Shade-Grown Coffee Is Unique

✦ Dual-Tier Forest Shade

Grown beneath towering silver oak, jackfruit, and wild fig trees. The filtered sunlight slows cherry maturation, allowing natural sugars and aromatics to develop to maximum density.

✦ Spice Companion Botany

Coffee shrubs share soil with wild black pepper vines, cardamom pods, cinnamon bark, and vanilla orchids, imparting natural aromatic terroir nuances into the bean.

✦ Cryogenic Stasis Roasting

We combine 400-year-old heritage harvesting with modern sub-zero liquid nitrogen quenching to lock volatile aromatics inside the bean matrix.
